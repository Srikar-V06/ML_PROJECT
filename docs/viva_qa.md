# Viva Q&A — Blocks 1, 2 & 3

> *Note on cohort evolution:* Questions 4–6 in Block 1 reflect P1's initial 200k-loan diagnostic run (`commit 4bdd5cf`); in the final P1/P2 handoff (`commit 2f9572e`), `lc_loan.csv.zip` borrower linkage was added to enforce **300,000 distinct verified borrowers** (`210k` Train / `45k` Val / `45k` Test) and a 50-field origination budget (`splits/p1p2_300k/`). Both sets of numbers are preserved below so nothing from Block 1 or Block 2 is lost.

## Block 1

1. **Why exclude Current?** Its terminal outcome is unknown. Calling it good would reward vintages with insufficient follow-up.
2. **Why 2016Q1?** It is the last quarter of the contiguous window passing the 90% 36-month resolution rule. The cutoff quarter achieved 93.41%.
3. **What remains censored?** Only 58.65% of 60-month loans at the cutoff were resolved. Early exits can have different risks from loans still active.
4. **What does 200k (and the final 300k handoff) represent?** The initial 200k cohort was a proportional sample of 938,540 eligible accepted and resolved loans; the final 300k cohort draws 300,000 distinct borrowers (`member_id` verified unique via `audit.parquet`, excluding prior experiment IDs in `excluded_loan_ids.parquet`). Rejected applicants have no observed outcome here.
5. **How close was the sample?** Maximum PSI was 0.0000643 and maximum annual rate gap 0.001029 in the initial draw (and verified in `artifacts/p1p2_300k/sampling_representativeness.csv` for the 300k draw). PSI uses binned distributions; continuous KS uses the empirical distributions.
6. **Can the same person appear twice?** In the raw public CSV `member_id` was blank, so loan IDs alone could not rule out repeat borrowers; in the 300k handoff, `src/data/splits.py:verify_borrowers` links `member_id` from `lc_loan.csv.zip` (`audit.parquet`) and asserts 1-to-1 borrower uniqueness across all 300,000 rows so zero borrowers straddle Train, Val, and Test.
7. **How do you catch leakage that looks predictive?** Inventory timing first, screen one feature at a time on Train and then deliberately contaminate a diagnostic fit. Its validation AUC was 0.99964.
8. **Why keep almost-empty variables?** Coverage can encode timing, product or borrower context. 29 were empty throughout Train in the 99-field inventory; in the 50-field handoff, 21 core underwriting fields plus the 29 highest-coverage varying numeric bureau fields are retained with fold-local missing indicators.
9. **Does missingness prove MNAR?** The two-proportion test measures an association in observed defaults. It cannot establish the missing-data mechanism or rule out vintage confounding.
10. **Why both OOT and expanding CV?** OOT tests transfer to later vintages. The four Train-only folds permit repeated model development without accessing final assessment rows.
11. **How does one scorecard point arise?** Each bin contributes −(20/ln 2) × coefficient × WoE. Add the intercept-adjusted base. Doubling good/bad odds adds 20 points.
12. **How did you prevent target leakage in WoE?** Fit boundaries, WoE, IV selection and logistic coefficients on Train. A value unseen in later data uses its learned bin or a neutral fallback.
13. **Why paired DeLong?** The same loans receive both scores. Paired placements retain covariance. Scorecard versus full logistic produced p=1.95e-07 (and p=1.66e-21 on the 45k 300k Test cohort); paired bootstrap also compares AP and Brier.
14. **What does a 10:1 cost ratio buy?** It values one false negative at ten false positives. The locked threshold balances missed-default losses against foregone interest on rejected good loans. Real exposure and foregone margin determine actual dollars.
15. **Can P2 apply the scorecard directly to Prosper?** Only 17 underwriting fields have mapped counterparts. A transfer experiment requires harmonized categories and units, a common-feature model and assessment of label and vintage shift.

## Block 2

1. **Why multiply installment by twelve?** Installment is a monthly payment and income is annual. The ratio expresses the share of reported income committed to this loan. A zero income leaves that ratio undefined.
2. **What does a tree split optimize?** The default Gini criterion favors a split whose children have purer outcome distributions. Entropy is another measure of class uncertainty. Minimum leaf size limits how small a segment can become, and cost-complexity pruning penalizes extra leaves.
3. **Why use out-of-bag estimates and expanding folds?** Each bootstrap tree omits some training loans, so those trees can score their omitted loans. This measures internal forest performance. Expanding folds answer a different question about performance on subsequently issued loans.
4. **Why can two importance rankings disagree?** Impurity importance rewards features that yield many useful training splits. Permutation importance measures validation AUC lost when one input is shuffled. A correlated substitute can keep the AUC high.
5. **Why might 95% explained variance still lose predictive information?** PCA learns orthogonal directions that capture variation in inputs. A low-variance direction can carry default signal, and rotation can turn a single underwriting cutoff into several tree splits. Compare each PCA model against its matched control on Val (and on Test, `pca_lightgbm_80` dropped to 0.7100 ROC vs 0.7425 raw LightGBM, whereas `pca_lr_95` stayed at 0.7349 vs 0.7372 raw Logistic).

## Block 3

1. **Why did you use chronological 90/10 Train-tail early stopping (`round(best_iter / 0.9)`) instead of early-stopping on Validation or using `HistGradientBoostingClassifier(early_stopping=True)`?**
   Passing `Val` as an `eval_set` leaks validation outcomes into tree counts (`best_iteration` in `artifacts/p3/tables/t3_val_summary.csv`, e.g., `1,704` for `xgb_hist`, `1,394` for `catboost`) and biases `Val` when selecting the Champion (`artifacts/p3/tables/frozen_val_leaderboard.csv`), choosing ensemble bases (`artifacts/p3/tables/ensembles_val.csv`), fitting calibrators (P4), and locking `5:1 / 10:1 / 15:1` thresholds. Meanwhile, `HistGradientBoostingClassifier(early_stopping=True)` draws a random internal split from `Train`, leaking future vintages into earlier loans. `fit_with_train_tail_early_stopping` fits on the earliest 90% of `Train` (`189,000` loans), selects `best_iter` on the latest 10% chronological tail (`21,000` loans; `staged_predict_proba` for `hgb`, `adaboost`, `gb_classic`), and refits on 100% of `Train` with `round(best_iter / 0.9)` (`1,893` rounds for `xgb_hist`, `1,549` for `catboost`), scaling tree count with training sample size while keeping `Val` untouched.

2. **Why did you treat P2's Optuna JSONs as Trial 0 warm starts rather than final hyperparameters?**
   P2's `artifacts/p1p2_300k/block2/search_*.json` files ran 3 trials on `≤3,000` rows/fold (`1.4%` of Train) without early stopping (`mean CV AUC 0.6800` for `lightgbm` to `0.6984` for `catboost`, `artifacts/p3/tables/handover_check.md`) — below P1's `logreg_baseline` (`0.7473` Val ROC-AUC). Enqueuing P2's `params` as Trial 0 inside P3's widened search (`25,000` rows/fold, `≤30` trials or `25-min` cap, `artifacts/p3/tables/recovery.csv`) raised mean expanding-fold CV AUC to `0.7121` (`lightgbm`), `0.7124` (`xgboost`), and `0.7131` (`catboost`), and Test ROC-AUC to `0.7425–0.7442` (`artifacts/p3/tables/benchmark.csv`).

3. **What did the 3-arm LightGBM missing-value study show, and why does median imputation barely affect GBDTs?**
   In `artifacts/p3/tables/lgbm_arms_comparison.csv` and `artifacts/p3/tables/benchmark.csv` (fixed tuned params: `num_leaves=9, lr=0.0137, min_child_samples=162`), `lgbm_native_flags` (`Val PR 0.4394`, `Test ROC 0.7425`, `Test PR 0.4243`), `lgbm_native` (`Val PR 0.4392`, `Test ROC 0.7424`, `Test PR 0.4245`), and `lgbm_imputed_flags` (`Val PR 0.4387`, `Test ROC 0.7420`, `Test PR 0.4247`) span `0.0005` Test ROC-AUC. Histogram GBDTs route `NaN` to whichever child maximizes split gain at every node, learning missingness interactions directly. Median imputation (`lgbm_imputed_flags`) loses `0.0004–0.0005` ROC-AUC on both Val and Test because missing bureau values are pooled with median-risk borrowers until an extra indicator split separates them.

4. **Why did `catboost` outperform one-hot GBDTs on Test ROC-AUC (`0.7442` vs `0.7429` `xgb_hist`, DeLong $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0033$)?**
   `CatBoostNativePreprocessor` passes the 5 categorical fields (`grade, sub_grade, home_ownership, verification_status, purpose`) as strings via `cat_features`. CatBoost computes permutation-ordered target statistics and builds symmetric oblivious trees (`depth=7`, `1,549` trees, `198,272` leaves in `artifacts/p3/tables/efficiency.csv`), avoiding sparse one-hot leaf fragmentation across `35` sub-grades and `14` loan purposes (`artifacts/p3/tables/significance.csv`).

5. **What did `class_weights`, `undersample`, and `SMOTE` do to ranking versus probability calibration, and why does that break cost-sensitive lending?**
   In `artifacts/p3/tables/imbalance_study.csv` and `artifacts/p3/tables/benchmark.csv` (`xgboost` and `catboost` on `210,000` Train rows), `class_weights` changed Test PR-AUC by `-0.0002` (`xgboost_imb_class_weights` `0.4262` vs `xgb_hist` `0.4264`) to `+0.0003` (`catboost_imb_class_weights` `0.4279` vs `catboost` `0.4276`), while `undersample` (`74,290` rows) cut Test PR-AUC to `0.4248` and `0.4256`. Both shifted log-odds by $+\ln(n_-/n_+) = +1.538$, inflating Val `mean_pred_prob` from `0.1801–0.1840` to `0.4490–0.4567` (Val base rate `0.2020`), raising Test Brier from `0.1395–0.1402` to `0.1985–0.2033`, and raising Test ECE from `0.0242–0.0291` to `0.2340–0.2440`. Applying the uncalibrated 10:1 cutoff $t^* = 1/(1+10) = 0.0909$ to `catboost_imb_class_weights` (Val-optimal cutoff `0.3121`) would reject almost all borrowers. Plain `SMOTE` inside `imblearn.pipeline.Pipeline` (`345,710` resampled rows, applied after imputation + one-hot inside Train only) yielded Val `mean_pred_prob = 0.1901–0.1902` and reduced Test PR-AUC to `0.4228` (`xgboost_imb_smote`) and `0.4259` (`catboost_imb_smote`), because Euclidean interpolation across binary `{0, 1}` dummies places synthetic defaults at fractional values (`0 < x < 1`) isolated by tree splits from real `{0, 1}` Test rows.

6. **How did you validate `lgbm_monotone`, why `fico_range_low` instead of `fico_mean`, and why did raw `annual_inc` violate monotonicity?**
   `artifacts/p1p2_300k/feature_audit.csv` retains `fico_range_low` as the sole FICO field (`fico_mean` does not exist). In `artifacts/p3/tables/monotone_sign_check.md` (`monotone_sign_deciles.csv`), all 7 candidate features had Train-decile `|Spearman ρ| ≥ 0.988`. In `artifacts/p3/tables/benchmark.csv` and `artifacts/p3/tables/significance.csv`, `lgbm_monotone` scored `Test ROC 0.7414` vs `0.7424` `lgbm_native` (`Δ = -0.0010`, DeLong `p_holm = 0.0023`) and `Test PR 0.4235` vs `0.4245` (`Δ = -0.0009`, paired bootstrap `p_holm = 0.5075`). In `artifacts/p3/tables/lgbm_monotone_ice_check.csv`, 6 constrained model inputs had `0 / 2,000` ICE violations (`70 / 2,000` steps `≤5.79e-4` on `annual_inc` under `monotone_constraints_method="advanced"`), whereas varying *raw* `annual_inc` violated monotonicity on `1,770 / 2,000` Val rows (`max_violation = 5.06e-2`) because raw income also changes `loan_to_income` (`+1`), `payment_to_income` (`+1`), and unconstrained `revolving_to_income` (`0`).

7. **Why couldn't you use `sklearn.ensemble.StackingClassifier`, and why did `stack_lr` reach `Test ECE = 0.0045`?**
   `StackingClassifier` calls `cross_val_predict`, which requires validation folds to partition `Train`; forward-chaining splits (`train_expanding_cv.parquet`) leave the earliest `42,000` loans without prior training rows, and standard K-fold leaks future vintages into past OOF scores. `src/run_block3_t6.py` refit `xgb_hist`, `catboost`, and `logreg_baseline` on each expanding fold's past loans to score `168,000` strictly out-of-time OOF rows (`artifacts/p3/stack/stack_lr_meta.json`). `LogisticRegression(C=1.0)` on OOF logits learned weights `0.6514` (`catboost`), `0.2554` (`logreg_baseline`), `0.1811` (`xgb_hist`), and intercept `+0.2685`, absorbing the upward default-rate shift (`17.69%` Train → `19.91%` Test) and reducing Test ECE from `0.0242` (`catboost`) to `0.0045` and Test Brier from `0.1395` to `0.1386` (`artifacts/p3/tables/ensembles_val.csv`, `artifacts/p3/tables/benchmark.csv`).

8. **How did you prevent Test snooping and control multiple-testing error?**
   `src/run_block3_f1_freeze.py` froze all 26 models, SHA-256 digests, 50-column `feature_order`, Val-locked thresholds, and the Val-chosen Champion (`stack_lr`, `artifacts/p3/tables/frozen_val_leaderboard.csv`) in `artifacts/p3/model_manifest.json`. `src/final_benchmark.py` verified every SHA-256, wrote `artifacts/p3/test_lock.json` (blocking any second run), and ran 1,000-resample paired bootstrap and DeLong tests (`artifacts/p3/tables/significance.csv`) with **Holm-Bonferroni step-down** adjusted `p_holm` across the 25 Champion-vs-all tests and 7 pre-registered pairs.

9. **Why report both `tracemalloc` and RSS in `efficiency.csv`, and why time slow refits at `n=1`?**
   `tracemalloc` tracks only Python's allocator, missing native C++ buffers in LightGBM, XGBoost, and CatBoost (`infer_tracemalloc_mb = 2.60–2.97 MB` vs subprocess `infer_rss_mb = 218.9–339.3 MB` in `artifacts/p3/tables/efficiency.csv`, converted from bytes on macOS `ru_maxrss`). Isolated subprocesses captured both metrics. Models refitting in `<120s` used the median of `3` refits (`4` threads, e.g., `xgb_hist` `13.44s`, `lgbm_native_flags` `14.80s`); models `≥120s` (`catboost` `126.65s`, `adaboost` `160.87s`, `stack_lr` `635.30s` OOF+meta) ran once (`n=1`) and are explicitly flagged in `n_train_runs` and `train_timing_note`.

10. **Which models sit on the Pareto frontier (`pareto_frontier.csv`), and what is the production recommendation?**
    In `artifacts/p3/tables/pareto_frontier.csv` and `artifacts/p3/tables/efficiency.csv` (`1`-thread inference), the calibrated non-dominated models are `grade_only` (`1.28 ms/1k`, `Test PR 0.3647`), `logreg_baseline` (`5.69 ms/1k`, `0.0051 MB`, `PR 0.4157`), `xgb_hist` (`12.57 ms/1k`, `13.44s` train, `0.396 MB`, `PR 0.4264`), and `catboost` (`19.44 ms/1k`, `126.65s` train, `1.552 MB`, `PR 0.4276`). Deploy **`xgb_hist`** for `<15 ms/1k` (`12.6 µs`/loan) latency SLAs and fast retraining, **`catboost`** if `19.44 ms/1k` is within budget (`+0.0013` ROC-AUC over `xgb_hist`, `p_holm = 0.0033`), **`stack_lr`** (`42.08 ms/1k`, `PR 0.4279`, `Brier 0.1386`, `ECE 0.0045`) when calibrated cutoff decisions dominate, or **`lgbm_monotone`** (`39.50 ms/1k`, `ROC 0.7414`) when feature monotonicity is mandated (after constraining `revolving_to_income`).
